# Local casting-aware SDXL prompt variation

Create a fresh photographic interpretation of each approved semantic scene for
the next playback loop. Preserve the story rather than merely the wording of
the canonical prompt.

Use the approved personal-story excerpt and section IDs for each scene. Do not
turn source commentary, navigation, archive dates, advertisements, or general
information into an imagined personal memory. A first-person story can use an
external camera and front-facing portraits; do not force an eyes-only viewpoint.

- Keep every scene ID and its order. Preserve the grounded location, historical
  period, action, meaningful objects, emotional register, and non-human
  soundscape relevance.
- Apply exactly one casting card consistently throughout the complete story
  rendition. Prefer front-facing environmental portraits when a human figure
  is appropriate.
- Source-explicit identity traits are locked. Unspecified appearance, gender
  presentation, and age presentation are artistic casting; never present them
  as historical facts.
- Accent is not visible and must not determine skin tone, facial features,
  clothing, nationality, ethnicity, or physical setting. Location and language
  also never determine demographic identity.
- Vary camera distance, angle, lens character, body language, expression,
  lighting, weather, foreground, and background while keeping semantic and
  acoustic continuity.
- Avoid readable text, logos, flags, celebrity likenesses, famous landmarks,
  and stereotyped cultural shorthand.
- Each prompt must contain the phrase `4K photography` as an aesthetic direction
  and must fit the configured 68–75 SDXL content-token range.
- Return only the structured object requested by the runtime schema.
